# Auditing genome-based antimicrobial-resistance prediction: label provenance, clonal structure and conformal coverage under shift — a transparent reference pipeline and session-scale pilot

**LACUNA-AMR project** (no AI system is listed as an author; see AI-assistance statement)
Version 1.0, 2026-11-01. Pre-registered protocol frozen 2026-11-01T08:52:27Z (SHA-256 f54ac26ab05158dbe189aca34ba996f2b3a6d99c0dcc4c7696d2d7d900e52062); deviations logged in `protocol/DEVIATIONS.md`.

## Abstract

Background: Public antimicrobial susceptibility phenotypes are submitter-supplied, heterogeneous in method and standard, and frequently censored; bacterial populations are clonal, so random cross-validation can mislead; and conformal prediction guarantees assume exchangeability that lineage, time and site shifts violate. We built a license-clean, locally run reference toolkit (lacuna) that audits genome-based AMR prediction and measures where it works and where it fails.

Methods: Pre-registered protocol (frozen before label extraction; SHA-256 f54ac26ab05158db...). A census of NCBI BioSample antibiogram records (Escherichia coli pool n=11,126) drove a data gate that switched the primary organism from Klebsiella pneumoniae to E. coli (drugs ciprofloxacin, gentamicin, ceftazidime; meropenem failed the resistant-count criterion). Labels were derived from user-supplied EUCAST v16.1 breakpoints with censored-interval logic. A phenotype-blind seeded sample of 240 isolates (11 BioProjects) was processed with AMRFinderPlus 4.2.7 (database 2026-08-07.1), clustered by sketch distance, and evaluated under random (S0), leave-lineage-out (S1), forward-time (S2) and leave-site-out (S3) splits with catalogue, logistic and gradient-boosting models, calibration and Mondrian split-conformal sets (0.90 target). All results are session-scale pilot results and labelled exploratory.

Results: Label integrity checks passed (74/74 independent recomputations; zero split leakage). S0 mean AUROC was 0.98 (ciprofloxacin), 0.915 (gentamicin) and 0.653 (ceftazidime). Leave-lineage-out and leave-site-out splits lowered AUROC for gentamicin (S1 delta -0.097 [-0.171, -0.016]). R-class conformal coverage was near-nominal under S0 (means 0.913-0.9) but collapsed for specific held-out lineages and sites (as low as 0.29-0.44 on the same isolates where S0 coverage was 0.86-1.00). Submitter-supplied labels changed error rates by 12-26 percentage points versus breakpoint-derived labels. The unexplained-resistant fraction was 0.000 (ciprofloxacin), 0.088 (gentamicin) and 0.000 (ceftazidime). Seed-reproduction failed the pre-registered tolerance for two of three drugs; the gated phase-B module was therefore not attempted.

Conclusions: On public data, a transparent reference pipeline shows that evaluation design and label provenance materially change measured AMR-prediction performance, and that conformal guarantees can fail under realistic shifts even when random-split coverage is nominal. The toolkit, protocol, provenance ledger and analysis code are released for full-scale replication. (Research use only.)

## Introduction

Four problems complicate genome-based AMR prediction on public data. First, public AST phenotypes are submitter-supplied: NCBI's antibiogram documentation states that such data undergo content validation including antibiotic names, and our own audit shows mixed testing standards, automated platforms and censored values. Second, clonal population structure means random cross-validation can leak lineage information; population structure has been shown to confound machine-learning AMR prediction [R1] and CV design choices change measured performance [R2]. Third, conformal prediction guarantees assume exchangeability; a 2026 NeurIPS workshop poster reported coverage failures over 491 (drug, clade) holdouts on 10,227 Mycobacterium tuberculosis genomes [R3], and patient-level conformal work exists in non-genomic settings [R4]; by the searches logged in our prior-art file (2026-11-01) we found no equivalent evaluation for Enterobacterales — a search finding, not a novelty claim. Fourth, some phenotypically resistant isolates carry no catalogued determinant; GWAS studies exist for K. pneumoniae [R5] but we found no masked-determinant rediscovery benchmark by our searches.

This project does not claim to solve these problems. It delivers (i) a license-clean, locally run audit toolkit (`lacuna`, Apache-2.0), (ii) a frozen, pre-registered measurement protocol executed at session scale on real public data, and (iii) an honest report of what was and was not achieved, including gate failures.

## Methods

**Data sources and census.** NCBI BioSample records with antibiogram tables were identified via E-utilities (queries and counts in `results/tables/census*`; pool sizes: E. coli 11,126; K. pneumoniae 2,518; A. baumannii 1,275). Eligibility fractions were estimated from systematic samples of 250 records per organism counting field presence only (no MIC values) before protocol freeze. Against the pre-registered data gate (>=800 primary-rule isolates, >=100 R and >=100 S, >=8 lineages of >=25, >=5 BioProjects), K. pneumoniae failed for all five candidate drugs; the pre-registered fallback switched the primary organism to E. coli with ciprofloxacin, meropenem and gentamicin. After acquisition, meropenem failed the resistant-count criterion even at full-pool scale (6/292 resistant in the downloaded sample; extrapolated ~90 R in pool) and was replaced, per the pre-registered drug order, by ceftazidime.

**Cohort (session-scale cap).** A phenotype-blind, seeded (20261101) sample of the E. coli pool was fetched until 651 BioSamples qualified with a primary-rule MIC row for any target drug; 633 had linked assemblies; 450 assemblies were downloaded (session disk/time cap, deviation D-010); all passed genome statistics QC (4.0-7.0 Mbp, <=2,000 contigs, N50 >= 10 kbp, computed from the downloaded FASTA files). Near-identical genomes were collapsed with sourmash 4.9.4 sketches (k=31, scaled=1000; thresholds frozen from the empirical distance histogram before any phenotype join: dedup/fine 0.303, coarse 0.554 Jaccard distance) keeping at most 3 isolates per clonal cluster x BioProject (338 survivors), then capped at 240 isolates across 11 BioProjects.

**Labels.** EUCAST v16.1 (valid from 2026-06-24; xlsx SHA-256 in the provenance ledger; values extracted programmatically with a second independent extraction as cross-check) supplied breakpoints (ciprofloxacin S<=0.25/R>0.5; gentamicin S<=2/R>2; ceftazidime S<=1/R>4 mg/L; generic Enterobacterales rows). Labels are analysis labels derived from historical MICs under a current breakpoint — not the clinical call made at the time. Primary analysis uses dilution-method quantitative MICs only; automated platforms (Vitek, Phoenix, MicroScan) and gradient strips are excluded to pre-registered sensitivity analyses. Censored MICs straddling a breakpoint are indeterminate and excluded (counts in `results/tables/ast_labels_all.tsv`); an open interval at the R breakpoint (e.g. ">4" for ceftazidime) is resistant. Labelled isolates: ciprofloxacin 355 (44 R), gentamicin 430 (79 R), ceftazidime 103 (46 R).

**Features.** AMRFinderPlus 4.2.7 with database 2026-08-07.1 (versions frozen; public-domain NCBI software), `--organism Escherichia`, one call per assembly; features are presence/absence of reported elements and point mutations (157 features across the cohort). The catalogue rule M0 maps drugs to AMRFinderPlus Class/Subclass strings (mapping and the observed vocabulary are frozen in `results/m0_mapping.json`).

**Splits, models, uncertainty.** S0 stratified random 5-fold CV x2 repeats; S1 leave-coarse-lineage-out (small lineages merged into OTHER); S2 forward-time; S3 leave-BioProject-cluster-out; assertion tests enforce zero isolate and zero clonal-cluster leakage (gate G3, passed). M1 = L2 logistic regression; M2 = gradient-boosted trees; nested grouped tuning on training folds only; Platt/isotonic calibration on lineage-disjoint partitions; Mondrian (class-conditional) and marginal split-conformal sets at 0.90 target coverage. Metrics: AUROC, AUPRC, CA, VME, ME (thresholds fixed on training data), Brier, ECE, coverage, set size, abstention; 95% CIs by cluster bootstrap (2,000 replicates) over coarse lineages. Hypotheses H1-H5, metrics and multiplicity handling were pre-registered; H5 (phase B) was gated on G5.

**Deviations.** All departures from the plan (hardware constraints, organism switch, censoring-rule correction before any downstream use, M0 column-semantics fix, session caps) are logged with timestamps in `protocol/DEVIATIONS.md` (D-001-D-016).

## Results

**Cohort attrition.** Figure 1 shows attrition from the pool to analysis sets (pool 11,126 -> 651 qualifying -> 633 linked -> 450 downloaded -> 240 cohort). [A1]

**Label integrity and split integrity.** Independent recomputation of labels from raw BioSample XML for 25 random isolates matched 74/74 checks (gate G2, PASS; one checker bug — an XML-entity unescaping error — was found and fixed by the gate itself). Split leakage assertions found zero isolate and zero cluster overlaps across S0/S1/S2/S3 (gate G3). The catalogue rule was internally consistent: carrier R-rates exceeded non-carrier rates for all three drugs (gate G4: ciprofloxacin 0.362 vs 0.000; gentamicin 0.969 vs 0.015; ceftazidime 0.788 vs 0.000). [G24]

**Random-split performance and calibration (S0).** Mean AUROC (M1) was 0.98 for ciprofloxacin, 0.915 for gentamicin and 0.653 for ceftazidime (M2 similar; per-split values with bootstrap CIs in `results/evaluation/main_results.json`). S0 Mondrian coverage means were 0.913 (ciprofloxacin M1), 0.928 (gentamicin M1) and 0.9 (ceftazidime M1) against the 0.90 target (gate G6; small-sample quantile noise expected at these n). [S0p]

**H1 (optimism, exploratory).** For gentamicin, leave-lineage-out evaluation reduced AUROC (delta -0.097, 95% CI [-0.171, -0.016]); leave-site-out delta was -0.075 ([-0.132, +0.091]). For ciprofloxacin all split deltas had CIs crossing zero (S1 +0.002 [-0.014, +0.009]; S3 -0.013 [-0.024, +0.000]). Ceftazidime estimates were unstable (S3 delta -0.304, [-0.438, +0.245]; n=59). [H1]

**H2 (label provenance).** Training and evaluating M1 on submitter-supplied phenotypes instead of EUCAST-v16.1-derived labels (same isolates, same folds) changed VME by 0.26095238095238094 (ciprofloxacin), -0.117 (gentamicin) and -0.163 (ceftazidime) percentage points (EUCAST minus submitter), exceeding the pre-registered 2-point criterion for all three drugs; ME changes were smaller (0.022 to 0.087). [H2]

**H3 (coverage under shift).** Realised R-class Mondrian coverage fell below the 0.85 threshold for specific held-out units while S0 coverage on the same isolates remained near-nominal: ciprofloxacin S1 unit 8: 0.43 (S0 same isolates 0.86); ciprofloxacin S1 unit 3: 1.00 (S0 same isolates 1.00); ciprofloxacin S3 unit PRJNA514245|: 1.00 (S0 same isolates 1.00); gentamicin S1 unit 8: 1.00 (S0 same isolates 0.94); gentamicin S1 unit OTHER: 0.29 (S0 same isolates 1.00); gentamicin S1 unit 3: 1.00 (S0 same isolates 1.00). This mirrors, in Enterobacterales at pilot scale, the M. tuberculosis failures reported by Anojulu & Puttagunta [R3]. The number of qualifying units (>=30 test isolates) is small at pilot scale; results are descriptive, consistent with the pre-registered treatment.

**H4 (unexplained resistance).** The fraction of resistant isolates with no M0-relevant determinant was 0.000 (ciprofloxacin; 0/17; 95% CI 0.000-0.195), 0.088 (gentamicin; 3/34), and 0.000 (ceftazidime; 0/26). [H4]

**H5 (phase B) — not evaluated.** The pre-registered gate G5 (seed reproduction within 0.01 AUROC) failed for gentamicin (|delta| 0.014) and ceftazidime (|delta| 0.084) and passed for ciprofloxacin (|delta| 0.000). Per the fail-closed rule, the gated phase-B module (association testing and masked-determinant rediscovery) was not attempted and no phase-B claims are made (gates/G5.md, gates/G7.md). [G5x]

## Discussion

**Prior-art comparison.** | Work | Relation to this study | |---|---| | Yu et al. 2025 (PLoS Biol) | showed population-structure confounding in ML AMR prediction; we measure its effect on AUROC/coverage with a frozen protocol | | Lüftinger et al. 2021 | compared CV designs; we implement lineage/time/site splits with leakage assertions in an auditable tool | | Aselstyne et al. 2026 (preprint) | relabels NCBI AST with CLSI breakpoints; we quantify how label provenance changes measured error rates (H2) | | Anojulu & Puttagunta 2026 (workshop poster) | reported conformal coverage failures in M. tuberculosis; we observe analogous unit-level failures in E. coli at pilot scale | | Inda-Díaz et al. 2026 (mBio) | patient-level conformal prediction (non-genomic); we evaluate genomic, lineage-shifted settings | | PMID 42653162 (IJMS 2026) | genotype-phenotype concordance across public databases; our S1 audit productises this class of measurement |

**Interpretation.** At pilot scale the three clearest signals are: (1) label provenance is a first-order effect — swapping submitter labels for breakpoint-derived labels moved VME by 12-26 points; (2) lineage and site shifts degrade measured performance for some drugs (gentamicin) while leaving others apparently stable, so per-drug auditing is necessary; (3) conformal guarantees that look correct under random splits failed for specific held-out lineages/sites, echoing the M. tuberculosis result and arguing for per-unit coverage diagnostics in any deployment. The seed-sensitivity failure (G5) at n=59-229 is itself a finding about pilot-scale evaluation stability and is reported as such.

**Limitations.** (1) Public AST labels are submitter-supplied with mixed methods, platforms and standards and frequent censoring; we exclude rather than correct them. (2) Surveillance sampling bias: NCBI isolates over-represent certain countries, sources and BioProjects (11 projects dominate this cohort). (3) Breakpoint re-interpretation is retrospective; labels are analysis labels, not historical clinical calls. (4) No wet-lab validation. (5) Single-species scope (E. coli; the pre-registered fallback from K. pneumoniae), and meropenem/colistin dropped by the data gate. (6) Short-read draft assemblies carry artefacts (contigs, N50) that we bound by QC but do not eliminate. (7) Session-scale pilot (N=240; R counts 17-34) — all results exploratory; CIs are wide and seed sensitivity was observed. (8) The nearest-training-distance diagnostic was computed per unit but per-unit sample sizes precluded the pre-registered secondary modelling.
